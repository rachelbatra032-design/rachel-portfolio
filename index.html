Help Pulse

Revenue Cloud help articles collect unhelpful votes faster than writers can read them. Each one is an HTAV. I wrote the triage rules that sort that pile into a shared backlog: act, don’t, or need more, then a fix type and a P0 to P3. The dashboard is how the team sees the queue. AI drafts a pass. A person still has to say yes. The version here is mock data only.

Role: Wrote the triage rules and built the dashboard

Agentforce Catalog

Product Catalog Management on Salesforce Revenue Cloud is how a business defines what it sells. As catalogs grow, the work is still done by hand. The project is a four-phase information architecture and an AI interaction model: Agentforce where the task is intent, embedded AI where it is repetition. The administrator keeps the last word on every suggestion.

Role: Information architecture, user flows, and the AI interaction model

Vikalp

Two Hindi print pamphlets for rural men aged 18–24 in Madhya Pradesh, so family planning is not only a woman’s job. Zero-parity men are asked to wait at least two years before the first child. Single-parity men are asked to space the second by at least three. Team ARSon, IPAS VIKALP, Development Communication Hackathon 2025. Close gate fold, Gond art, and a behavioural research note written before the make.

Role: Leaflet design and behavioural research

Wasila

Harvard Policy Hackathon 2025, with Arz Taneja, Avantika Rajaram, and Sakshi Singh. Credit exclusion in Jammu and Kashmir is not a missing pile of capital. Banks are there. First-time entrepreneurs are not. The brief treats that as a chain that breaks (origination, assessment, disbursal, repayment, repeat credit) and proposes Wasila as the missing layer: a holding company that scores informal activity, verifies locally, and lets non-bank lenders meet a pre-scored borrower without taking over the loan book.

Role: Harvard Policy Hackathon brief, four-person team

Oaxaca

With Arz Taneja. A St. Stephen’s paper on gender wage discrimination in India, using IHDS 2005 and 2011. A modified Mincer wage function, then Blinder–Oaxaca: how much of the gap is what men and women bring, and how much is the same traits paying differently in a city and in a village. In 2011 the log wage gap is 0.50. Most of it is coefficients.

Role: Co-author

Internalising Externalities

A solo theory essay from Pigou through to carbon markets. Economic externalities are easier to place than social ones. Education, brain drain, a network that can be support or abuse: putting a number on those is almost impossible, which is why the institutional tool has to change with the externality instead of forcing one instrument through every case.

Role: Author

Diaspora for Development

With Maria Joseph. An Indian perspective on the diaspora as a development channel, not only a pipe for money home. Two modes of engagement sit in the models: remittances, and knowledge. The paper asks who counts as the diaspora, under what conditions, and what would improve the engagement that already exists.

Role: Co-author

Mass Incarceration in the US

With Arz Taneja and Sakshi Singh. NLSY 1979–2020. The American prison would matter less if it only held the people inside it. The paper reads incarceration as an institution that concentrates inequality by class and race, and as a credential that follows people after release.

Role: Co-author
