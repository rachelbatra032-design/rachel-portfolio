Diaspora for Development

An Indian perspective. With Maria Joseph. The diaspora as a development channel, remittances and knowledge flows, not only a pipe for money home.

WithMaria Joseph, Azim Premji University
VenueRamjas Economic Review
MethodA model of remittances, a model of knowledge flows, then policy

The frame

The Indian diaspora is large and still growing. India is the largest recipient of remittances. It also stands to gain from knowledge flows if engagement is designed for brain gain rather than only for transfers. The paper asks what, specifically, in this diaspora moves either stream, and what kind of engagement would actually propel them.

Two old questions sit underneath: who counts as the diaspora, and under what conditions; and what the impact is on home and host. People carry cultural baggage. Many in this diaspora have kept social and cultural links with the country of origin even while they excel elsewhere. Migration itself is layered, different periods, different communities, different destinations. India now has a ministry for this. Views on whether migration helps development still diverge.

What the paper does

A literature pass on the economics of the Indian diaspora, then on the two modes of engagement that matter here: remittances, and knowledge. Then two models built from traits specific to this diaspora. Then a read of those models for policy, what would improve the engagement that already exists.

Money home is one channel. Knowledge that becomes brain gain is the other. Neither moves just because a ministry exists.

A shorter related op-ed, “The Dynamism of the Indian Diaspora,” sits on the same line of work.
