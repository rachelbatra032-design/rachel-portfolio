WASILA: Bridging Credit Gaps

A policy brief on expanding non-bank credit for first-time entrepreneurs in Jammu and Kashmir. The constraint is the chain, not the stock of capital.

WithArz Taneja, Avantika Rajaram, Sakshi Singh
BriefHarvard Policy Hackathon 2025 · Problem A
PlaceJammu and Kashmir
ProposalState-sponsored SPV as a Microfinance Holding Company

The paradox

J&K has entrepreneurial activity and relatively strong banks. Credit–deposit ratio above 60%. NPAs around 3.3%. More than 80% of disbursements still go through banks, and a large share through one institution. First-time and new-to-credit entrepreneurs stay out.

Startup Policy 2024–27 puts enterprise on the agenda. Exclusion persists because bank tools — collateral, papers, a credit history, a rigid EMI — are built for a formal economy and applied to an informal one. J&K Bank has used SHG participation for some lending. That does not reach everyone. Creditworthiness at entry is what the system cannot see.

Where the chain breaks

Origination → assessment → disbursal → repayment → repeat credit. People exit early. No single lender can fix the whole path.

Origination

Bank-led, thin non-bank and fintech presence. Informal, home-based, or geographically scattered enterprises never enter the funnel. Last-mile costs are high. Digital-only models are not trusted enough to replace a branch.

Assessment

Collateral, historical credit, standard documents. First-time and informal businesses have real activity and weak paper. Seasonal income, fragmented records, thin digital trails. Rejection at assessment is often an inability to read cash flow, market participation, or community validation.

Disbursal and repayment

Loan design ignores irregular cash. Formal institutions have little reason to redesign the product. Lenders lack soft enforcement — community accountability, peer checks, schedules that move with the season. Borrowers fear debt and unforgiving EMIs. The gap is risk perception versus risk reality.

Repeat credit

Each cycle is a new application. No automatic limit for a good book. Enterprises stay permanently small.

What Wasila is

A neutral infrastructure layer. Lenders keep their own books. Wasila does not pretend the answer is more bank branches.

The brief proposes a state-sponsored special purpose vehicle structured as a Microfinance Holding Company. Unified intake. Alternative scoring. Community verification. A digital lender–borrower marketplace. Information asymmetries fall; origination risk for non-bank lenders falls; there is a path toward formal finance. Lender autonomy stays.

De-risk origination

NBFCs, impact investors, DFIs, CSR-linked capital stay out because origination is expensive, data is fragmented, verification is weak, and governance risk is real in a post-conflict setting. The holding company mediates. It separates verification and governance from the capital. Lenders can originate without carrying the local monitoring load alone.

Let non-bank capital in

A marketplace where lenders meet pre-scored borrowers. Scoring, validation, and monitoring sit in the infrastructure. Capital stays diversified. Greenfield work needs patient, still-disciplined money.

Score the informal economy

NSS 2015–16: on the order of 413,250 MSMEs in the region, and about 400,000 craftspeople, a large share still unregistered. Micro and nano firms are scattered and seasonal. Documentation-heavy, collateral-based scores do not travel. The second recommendation is a scoring frame that can read non-traditional signals.
