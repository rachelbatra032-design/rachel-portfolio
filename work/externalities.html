Internalising Externalities

A socio-economic analysis. Solo theory essay, St. Stephen’s, December 2023; later in Economic and Political Weekly.

FormTheory essay
Starts fromPigou, Arrow
MoveClassify the externality before picking the instrument

The question

Net-zero talk is often just internalisation with a new name. A firm with a pollution spillover can enter a carbon market and trade the gas. Economic externalities are easier to place than social ones. Education has long tails on quality of life. Brain drain is an international spillover. A social network can be support or a platform for abuse. Putting a number on those services and disservices is almost impossible, which is why the institutional role has to change with the context.

Public goods sit next to this. If society at large benefits, the language is public good; if only a part does, the language is externality. That line is still vague. The essay tries to make it usable.

Attributes before instruments

Ten attributes, five headings, so a carbon market and a social-network spillover are not forced through one tool.

  • Occurrence: certain or uncertain. Uncertain ones are hard to measure because the distribution moves.
  • Source: identifiable or not; one source or many.
  • Space: local or not.
  • Time: sporadic or regular.
  • Technology: preventable or not; unidirectional or reciprocal; something you can insulate from, or not.

Who internalises, and why

Firms internalise when they can cut total cost or protect brand. The state steps in when welfare is the point: Pigouvian tax and subsidy, cap-and-trade, quantity regulation. Coase is on the table. So is the objection that assigning property rights is the hard part at macro scale.

The private sector wants an economic advantage. The public sector wants social well-being. The same word needs different tools once you care who is doing the internalising.
